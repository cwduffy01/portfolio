## 3.15.25 - Cutting / Assembly

I cut out the pieces on the Dorian laser at Asmbly earlier today. The results turned out great!

![DF90EE56-1879-47A4-84DC-13DF05D9F31C](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/DF90EE56-1879-47A4-84DC-13DF05D9F31C.jpeg)

During my first pass, one of the stewards, Danny Miller, swung by and gave me some great advice. First, in lightburn, the best layers to use for tracing the outline of a piece of material are T1 and T2. These are tool layers and will *never* show up in the cut. They work really well for setting the origin of the piece. 

Second, he had really great advice for protecting the acrylic from the laser and getting the best finish. He recommended peeling off the paper on only one side of the material. Otherwise, the layer will flash back into the material when it hits the honeycomb and leave marks. 

![EC6E2E38-67C5-48B2-9185-EB8C749BF95B_1_201_a](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/EC6E2E38-67C5-48B2-9185-EB8C749BF95B_1_201_a.jpeg)

The paper on the bottom diffuses the laser and leaves a better finish. As for the top, a thin layer of dish soap applied to the top with my fingers did the trick. On my second cut, the pieces looked much nicer.

I assembled the pieces together first with E6000 glue which was NOT the move, so I then switched to super glue. The results turned out alright!

![2C84DD8A-5AE6-4DFC-8313-6D6BC4ACDB81](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/2C84DD8A-5AE6-4DFC-8313-6D6BC4ACDB81.jpeg)
![5E19E539-7B7F-45A4-9620-87BA802C0408](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/5E19E539-7B7F-45A4-9620-87BA802C0408.jpeg)

After assembly, I have a few thoughts on how I should proceed:
- The tolerances between the pieces are a little bit wiggly. As a result, the fit isn't the most ideal. The feet aren't the most level with the surface of my table, and the pads aren't the most flush with the cube's faces. I could fix this in two ways:
  - Tighten the tolerances with some testing and figure out how to standardize them with all parts in Fusion 360
  - Design the stand such that the fit of the pieces isn't crucial. In the original design, the cube sits in an equilateral triangle
- The glue is visible after it dries. Danny suggested using acrylics glue or acrylics dissolved in some kind of solvent to bond the pieces together
- The pads, should they remain a design feature, should probably match the shape of the cube. The pads for this design should be rotated 45°.
- The design should be a bit taller and have more form. Would it look better with bigger cubes?

I really like these shapes as a concept for the next design, maybe I'll give it a shot?

## 3.17.25 - A Much Fancier Rendition

So, I gave it a shot. 

Here is the design of version 2 of the cube stand:

![Pasted-image-20250328134454](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/Pasted-image-20250328134454.png)

For this design, I modeled the cube itself first, which has the same size as my 7x7x7. Then, in the first sketch, I constrained the pad notch to fit flush to the cube's surface. The rest of the modeling for the curves was done free-hand with the Fit Point Spline sketch tool. The curves were mirrored and adjusted for the decorative pieces. 

![Pasted-image-20250328133734](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/Pasted-image-20250328133734.png)

I extruded, moved, and repeated the pieces via circular pattern to create the assembly. The pads were also added to the support pieces. The pads were made to be diamonds this time to match the cube's profile. 

Then, I created a sketch on the lower face of the notch for the ring joint, projected all of the necessary geometry, and extruded it. The design was complete. All I had left to do was create the dxf files. Just like last time, I created a sketch on each relevant face of the pieces and projected only the geometry that I wanted cut. 

One of the things I wanted to improve on from the last cut was better tolerances. I wanted the pieces to fit together pretty seamlessly. I created offset curves in the notches of each piece to easily adjust the tolerance, but to find out what tolerance I should aim for, I decided to do a series of tests on a piece of scrap acrylic to find the best measurement for both kinds of connecting pieces. 

![F7363B2E-3355-4BE1-8B22-88CE65E8BD34_1_201_a](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/F7363B2E-3355-4BE1-8B22-88CE65E8BD34_1_201_a.jpeg)
![58E9939F-BB9E-4B30-BEFF-598E699793A9](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/58E9939F-BB9E-4B30-BEFF-598E699793A9.jpeg)
![98C3A8AF-3E11-49DE-8BE5-E7458DDDEA28](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/98C3A8AF-3E11-49DE-8BE5-E7458DDDEA28.jpeg)

I settled on -0.25mm for the notches for the final cut since it was the tightest fit without being overkill, according to my tests. This means that if the pieces are modeled to fit flush to one another, the notch width should be decreased by 0.25mm, or 0.25mm / 2 on each side. I adjusted the files accordingly and exported the dxfs, brought them into Lightburn, and prepared them to cut!

It was at this point that I realized that something was horribly wrong with my files. For some reason, the curves in all dxf files had duplicated and split into small line segments. I tried my best to clean these up by grouping the lines that were supposed to stay and trimming the rest. It wasn't really working. My greatest concern was that the laser would try to cut over those curves more than once, which wouldn't really damage anything, but it was unnecessary. 

After trying my best to clean the files with no success, I resorted to crossing my fingers, starting the cut, and hoping for the best. After all, my material was just $3 and I could easily acquire more. 

![F6A007BB-E800-4360-BE04-330E379AD50E](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/F6A007BB-E800-4360-BE04-330E379AD50E.jpeg)

The results were anything but "the best." The laser just cut the line segments without connecting them, meaning the pieces couldn't even be removed from the acrylic, unless I popped them out with a lot of force. The ones that popped out either cracked or popped out with sharp pieces of excess acrylic around the edges. 

Luckily, I was able to pop out the four unique parts and put them together, only to realize that the tolerances were still too tight, and the notches on the ring joint were a bit shallow. I also cut my fingers a few times. Acrylic is sharp!

![5CE000DF-1DC0-4640-BBF9-8084C7EB1935](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/5CE000DF-1DC0-4640-BBF9-8084C7EB1935.jpeg)

Overall, this cut was a complete failure, but I learned a lot. After reflecting on it, I suspected that the sketch splines were complicated enough to confuse the "Project" tool in Fusion. My guess is somehow, when I projected the curves into a new sketch, it copied the geometry incorrectly and split it into a bunch of line segments. It wasn't an issue with the last design version since all of the lines were straight. 

Since I was bored and had an idle 3D printer, I decided to print out the model to check the fit, size, and design. 

![4AADBC55-4286-4800-9DCE-ED3F5B3F8CC0](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/4AADBC55-4286-4800-9DCE-ED3F5B3F8CC0.jpeg)

Unlike the final product, the 3D print is not made of solid plastic, but the support curves were flexing a LOT. It didn't seem like it would support the weight of the cube at all. A design change would need to be made. 

So here are the improvements that I would like to make for version 2
- Make the spines wider. This will fix two issues: bending under the weight of the cube and shallow notches in the ring joint.
- Add a spoke joint at the top of the model to make the support curves more rigid. 
- Fix the dxf files. I have to find some kind of way to process the files with the offset notches WITHOUT projecting the geometry in Fusion.
- Change the tolerance from -0.25mm to -0.15mm and hope that the fit is much better. The fit was pretty good in the testing I did. 

I think I shall name this model Dolores.

## 4.10.25 - Edna v2

After refining the design just a liiiiitle bit, I think I've arrived to something I like:

![Pasted-image-20250410145049](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/Pasted-image-20250410145049.png)

I realized that with all of the dimensioning I did for the feathers, the radii were still out of whack. Some feathers bowed a bit more outward, while others curved more inward. I also altered the silhouette a little bit to tighten the "waist" just a bit, plus the top and bottoms of the silhouette go a bit more outward. Also, I changed the foot design to curve along with the bottom-most feather before bowing outwards. I also created joints to hold everything together. I also changed the material thickness to be 2.95mm instead of 1/8" since that was the measured thickness of the acrylic sheet I bought. I also changed this assembly from 15 pieces to 12 pieces so I don't use as much material. I also changed the notch tolerance from 0.15mm to 0.20mm. 

So yeah, lots of minor modifications to v2, which ended up taking a while. But patience is key. I think even with the subtleties, this looks notably better than v1. I also found what I think the best method for modifying notch tolerances is!!
1. Create a sketch on each profile that needs to be cut and select the Project Geometry tool. Instead of projecting each line/curve, project the entire FACE. You'll end up getting an outline trace of the face you plan to cut without any extra geometry. 
2. For each notch, select the notch's walls and use the offset command to shrink the notch profile by `tolerance/2`. The notch will now be `tolerance` less than it was. Then, convert the original lines to construction geometry. Join the points at the end of the tolerance-adjusted notch with the rest of the profile. Proper constraints will allow a filled shape when `tolerance` is adjusted. 
3. Export each tolerance-adjusted sketch to DXF. In the export menu, I converted to inches (since it's the default for illustrator), unchecked "Points," and unchecked "Construction Geometry." This gave me a clean DXF with only the cut lines.

I tried this process with Dolores and ended up with much cleaner files.

## 4.20.25 - Cynthia v1

Consider this project officially unshelved (that didn't take long).

![5C3AB4B5-50F8-4A45-AACE-B1C15DE96336](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/5C3AB4B5-50F8-4A45-AACE-B1C15DE96336.jpeg)

As you can tell by the "YES." written underneath the sketch, I felt like I was onto something with this one. 

I wanted to break free from my obsession with curves and pursue something with straight edges. I feel like this concept, as simple as it is, still looks quite elegant. I have yet to see if I can make one of these without an hourglass silhouette but for now, I'm in pursuit of glamour and opulence. 

As you can see by the affirmative "yes" written underneath the sketch, this concept is the one. The challenge is that there are multiple ways that this kind of silhouette could be sculpted. I produced some preliminary designs to see if any of them stuck. 

![Pasted-image-20250422003124](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/Pasted-image-20250422003124.png)

In this one, the support pieces actually pass by each other near the center of the sculpture. I designed the pieces to pass through the middle initially, but before duplicating them, I shifted them sideways by a few millimeters. Honestly, I think that's okay.

I think that this is a very compelling design aesthetic. All of my other pieces have been sculpted from the center outwards. This is quite different, which is a good thing. I was looking for an aesthetic that would separate this design from the others. 

I also made a similar version in which the pieces pinch towards the middle but don't cross through the middle. 

![Pasted-image-20250422003209](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/Pasted-image-20250422003209.png)

This was originally meant to be a backup in case the other version was too difficult to implement. I liked both versions. Upon asking Justin, my design consultant, he indicated that he loved the design concept of the first. So now this one is a backup again.

These are both very bare bones models, and they need some more oomph to them. I'll look into adding oomph at a later time.

## 6.11.25 - The Cut

The cut went... interesting...

After finding the right the laser settings, I did some test cuts on the pieces with some very unfavorable results. First, the joint that was holding the pieces together was burning. Here is how the pieces were designed:

![Pasted-image-20250612234846](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/Pasted-image-20250612234846.png)

This is what the tolerance-adjusted sketch for this piece looks like. This sketch was exported as a DXF and cut on the laser:

![Pasted-image-20250613000227](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/Pasted-image-20250613000227.png)

You'll notice that I connected the tolerance-adjusted notches with straight lines to the curves. When I was cutting this piece, I noticed that the laser was dwelling on these straight lines for a little too long. The flashback from these cuts, in some areas, ended up melting the material. As shown below:

![5AB25C5B-1FF1-4EAA-BE58-94E94F9D8EB3](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/5AB25C5B-1FF1-4EAA-BE58-94E94F9D8EB3.jpeg)

To fix this, I had to re-design the pieces. After creating the tolerance-adjusted notches, I created new curves that connected directly to the notches' endpoints, rather than lines connected to the projected curves. I set the radius of the new curves to be equal to the original curves (which are shown as dotted lines below). 

![Pasted-image-20250612235332](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/Pasted-image-20250612235332.png)

These cut with little to no issues.

Still, after these modifications, the notches in my test pieces were a bit too tight, and they kept breaking at the notches. After taking another look at the Fusion document, I realized the material thickness was misconfigured. I had to do a few more test cuts, adjusting the tolerance between each, before arriving to something that worked well. 

Then, I hoped for the best and cut all the pieces. I took them home to clean and assemble. This was the final result:

![5612FDEA-9257-4B34-AAC1-F2D21C9958C5](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/5612FDEA-9257-4B34-AAC1-F2D21C9958C5.jpeg)

I think it looks awesome! Two of the pieces broke during assembly but I managed to save them during glue-up. I'm really thrilled with how this turned out. It looked better than I could have imagined it in Fusion. 

I managed to keep all my cuts on one sheet of material, even though I bought two. Here's my material utilization (the smudged finish is residual dish soap from pre-treating the material):

![4D8185B7-9733-475D-9DD5-60717F57E6FA_1_201_a](/images/projects/rubiks-cube-stands/4D8185B7-9733-475D-9DD5-60717F57E6FA_1_201_a.jpeg)

I'm pretty stoked that I managed to keep everything on one sheet!
